## 1. How do Radix, Tailwind v4, Open Color, Material 3/HCT, Carbon, Primer, Apple HIG and Leonardo structure dark-UI scales, and which map best onto background/panel/tile/raised/separator/text tiers?

### Takeaway
Radix Colors is the closest structural fit: a 12-step scale where each step has a fixed job (1 app bg, 2 subtle bg, 3 element bg, 4 hover, 5 pressed, 6 subtle border, 7-8 stronger borders, 9 solid, 10 solid hover, 11 low-contrast text, 12 high-contrast text), with dark-mode values published and APCA-guaranteed text steps. Carbon's layer model (background -> layer-01/02/03) and Material 3's surface-container tiers are the other good analogues for the tile/raised stacking; Tailwind and Open Color are raw ramps with no roles.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Tier mapping (Radix Slate dark as reference): BACKGROUND ~ step 1 (#111113), PANEL ~ step 2 (#18191b), TILE ~ step 3 (#212225), TILE_RAISED ~ step 4 (#272a2d), TRACK ~ step 5 (#2e3135), SEPARATOR ~ step 6 (#363a3f), TEXT_TERTIARY ~ step 9/10 (#696e77-#777b84), TEXT_SECONDARY ~ step 11 (#b0b4ba), TEXT ~ step 12 (#edeef0). - Material 3 surface-container tiers (lowest/low/container/high/highest) and Carbon's layer-01/02/03 both support the same "each nesting level is one step lighter" rule the dashboard already follows (BACKGROUND < PANEL < TILE < TILE_RAISED); Carbon's per-layer border tokens argue for making SEPARATOR depend on the surface it sits on rather than a single value.
- The project's current neutrals are a cool blue-grey (hue ~254-258, chroma 0.007-0.013 (computed)), matching Radix "Slate"/Primer "neutral" family rather than pure gray; staying in that family keeps the "Grafite" look.
- Tailwind's 400 accents are not lightness-matched across hues (yellow-400 L 85% vs red-400 L 70%), so Tailwind is not by itself a solution for the "no semantic colour dominates" goal; Radix steps 9/11 are likewise tuned per hue.
- Open Color is light-theme oriented (its 5-6 steps are mid-tones) and is of limited use on near-black; Apple's palette is the origin of the current accents (PINK #FF375F is exactly Apple dark pink; the others are Apple-like), consistent with the theme file's note about watchOS.
- Licence compatibility: Radix MIT and Leonardo/Material utilities Apache 2.0 are fine for copying values/algorithms into a private or open-source repo; colour hex values themselves are generally not copyrightable, but attribute Radix if reproducing its scale verbatim.

## 2. OKLCH/OKLab-based palette generation: perceptual uniformity, equal-lightness accents, tools

### Takeaway
OKLCH lets you fix lightness (L) and chroma (C) and vary only hue so no semantic accent is brighter or "louder" than another; the project's current accents are close but not equal (OKLCH L spans 61-88, computed). All of this can be done in stdlib Python (OKLab is ~25 lines of matrix maths), with hex as the output, which fits the "generated by code" philosophy; chroma must be reduced to stay inside sRGB.

### Cited Findings
- Tailwind v4 moved its whole palette to OKLCH (values above) — [tailwindcss theme.css](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tailwindlabs/tailwindcss/main/packages/tailwindcss/theme.css)
- Tools in the OKLCH ecosystem: oklch.com (interactive picker/converter showing sRGB and P3 gamut limits); Harmonizer (Evil Martians; OKLCH + APCA, consistent chroma and contrast across hues, standalone or Figma plugin); a palette generator by Roman Shamin (OKLCH variables for primitives, text, borders, surfaces from a brand colour, generates dark mode); apcach (JS lib, contrast-controlled OKLCH, APCA and WCAG, outputs OKLCH/HEX/RGB/P3); Polychrom (Figma contrast plugin with APCA). — [Evil Martians: Exploring the OKLCH ecosystem](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/exploring-the-oklch-ecosystem-and-its-tools)
- Gamut caveat: an oklch() value is relative to the sRGB gamut per CSS; pasting oklch into Figma's HEX field misbehaves with a Display P3 file (as of 28 May 2025) — [Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/exploring-the-oklch-ecosystem-and-its-tools)
- Huetone: described by third parties as an OKLCH-native scale tool with even perceptual steps and manual per-shade control; whether it shows APCA could not be confirmed from the retrieved snippets. Atmos lists APCA support in its comparison table. Leonardo is the contrast-first alternative. — [OpenReplay colour tools (de)](https://blog.openreplay.com/de/nützliche-farb-tools/); [Atmos vs Huetone](https://atmos.style/alternatives/huetone)
- Leonardo is contrast-ratio-driven and Apache 2.0 licensed — [adobe/leonardo](https://github.com/adobe/leonardo)

### Inferences
- Computed OKLCH of current accents on TILE #181B1F: GREEN L76.4 C0.178 h154; YELLOW L87.9 C0.162 h91; ORANGE L74.8 C0.176 h55; RED L66.9 C0.218 h29; CYAN L77.7 C0.127 h227; BLUE L61.2 C0.210 h261; PURPLE L67.6 C0.213 h304; AMBER L78.6 C0.149 h71; PINK L65.8 C0.232 h16; THROTTLE #2BFF88 L87.9 C0.224; BRAKE #FF2D2D L64.5 C0.241. Spread of ~27 L points: YELLOW and THROTTLE are the brightest; BLUE/PINK/BRAKE are the dimmest. Pedals being the brightest and most chromatic items is intentional per theme.py ("o maior destaque do dash").

- Candidate generated neutral ladder (computed, hue 255, C 0.010): BG L14.5 #080A0E, PANEL L17.5 #0E1115, TILE L20.5 #14171C, RAISED L24 #1C2024, TRACK L27 #23272B, SEP L29 #282C30. Candidate text (computed): TEXT L80 #BABEC3, SEC L70 #9B9FA3, TER L56 #717579, GEAR L97 #F4F5F7.

## 3. Contrast for at-a-glance reading: WCAG 2.x vs APCA for small numeric text on near-black; automotive minimums; halation of pure white

### Takeaway
WCAG 2.x ratios overstate contrast on dark backgrounds and say nothing about font weight; APCA Lc is polarity-aware and size/weight-dependent, with Lc 75 as the body-text floor and Lc 90 preferred for fluent text. WCAG 3 is still a draft in 2026 and APCA is not confirmed as part of it, so use APCA as an internal design target and WCAG 2 ratios as the compliance cross-check. The project's current text tiers pass comfortably except TEXT_TERTIARY.

### Cited Findings
- APCA guidance: Lc 90 preferred for fluent body text at >= 14px/400; Lc 75 minimum for body-text columns at 18px/400; Lc 60 minimum for non-body content text; Lc 45 for large heavy text (36px normal / 24px bold); Lc 30 absolute minimum for any other text (placeholder/disabled). The apca-w3 reference allows lowering by Lc 15 for sub-fluent text and 25 for spot text, never below Lc 30. Table state "current as of May 27, 2022", base algorithm 0.0.98G. — [apca-w3 (Myndex)](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-w3); [Myndex APCA model at W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/GL/task-forces/silver/wiki/Visual_Contrast_of_Text_Subgroup/APCA_model)
- APCA returns a signed value (about -108 to +106); negative = light text on dark background; sign tracks polarity, not strength — [apca-w3](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-w3)
- WCAG 3 is an incomplete draft in 2026 (most recent draft found dated March 2026; Bronze/Silver/Gold conformance model; recommendation not expected before ~2028 per one secondary source). Sources conflict on APCA: a 2024 co-chair update said APCA is not in the current draft; a 2026 explainer says WCAG 3 explores newer contrast approaches. — [W3C WCAG 3 Introduction](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG3); [WCAG 3 is not ready yet](https://www.yatil.net/blog/wcag-3-is-not-ready-yet); [webability](https://www.webability.io/blog/wcag-3-0-explained)
- WCAG 2.x thresholds (as relayed): normal text 4.5:1 AA, 7:1 AAA; large text 3:1; non-text UI 3:1. Don't rely on colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1). — [Apple HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color) (relayed via fetch tool; unverified)
- ISO 15008:2017 sets minimum image-quality/legibility requirements for in-vehicle displays viewed while driving, technology-independent; excludes coding/format, HUDs, quasi-static info (units, on/off indicators). A revision (ISO/AWI 15008) is in development since Nov 2023. Contrast/font-size chapters reference ISO 4513 and apply to passenger vehicles. Numeric thresholds are paywalled. SAE J1757/1 covers ambient-light display contrast measurement and gives only recommended (non-mandatory) values. — [ISO 15008:2017 listing](https://iteh.es/catalog/standards/iso/d07adfe7-f551-4557-bb09-7236d8491cf6/iso-15008-2017); [ISO AWI 15008](https://genorma.com/en/standards/iso-awi-15008); [SAE J1757/1](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/J1757/1_200704)
- Halation: bright text on dark can bloom/glow, attributed to wider pupils making astigmatic defocus more visible; claimed prevalence of astigmatism varies (30% to ~50%, unverified); pure #FFFFFF on #000000 (21:1) is called harsh; common advice is a dark grey background (#121212 or #1A1A1A) and off-white text (#E0E0E0/#D4D4D4). Evidence is mostly design guidance and anecdote, not rigorous studies; NN/g-type reviews cited by Trimble say light mode tends to be better for normal-vision users. — [Trimble Modus dark mode](https://modus.trimble.com/foundations/dark-mode); [vale.rocks](https://vale.rocks/micros/20260514-0436); [All About Vision](https://allaboutvision.com/conditions/computer-vision-syndrome/digital-eye-strain/is-dark-mode-better-for-eyes)

## 4. Colour semantics in motorsport displays: flags, steering-wheel conventions, iRacing/SimHub, colour-vision-deficiency safety

### Takeaway
The flag colours (yellow caution, red stop, green clear, blue let faster car pass, white slow vehicle/last lap) are a stable cross-sim vocabulary and are mirrored by wheel LED/flag-LED conventions, so the dashboard's alert colours should avoid re-using them for unrelated meanings (the project reserves orange for alert and cyan for limiter, which keeps clear of them). Red/green delta pairs are the weakest CVD point: computed separation collapses under deuteranopia; fixes are lightness separation, shape/sign redundancy, or a blue/orange alternative.

### Cited Findings
- Flag meanings: yellow = hazard, slow, no overtaking (double yellow = serious hazard, be ready to stop); red = session suspended, return to pit; green = all clear; blue = faster car approaching, let it by; white = much slower vehicle on that sector; black-and-white chequered = end of session. Black flag not covered by retrieved sources. — [PlanetF1 flags](https://www.planetf1.com/features/f1-flags-explained); [Formula1-dictionary flags](https://www.formula1-dictionary.net/f1-racing-flags/); [FOTW](https://crwflags.com/FOTW/FLAGS/xf-auto.html)
- F1 steering wheels carry a GPS marshalling-light display with lights coloured to match the flag shown in the sector — [GPFans F1 flags explained](https://www.gpfans.com/us/f1-news/1018369/f1-flags-explained)
- Sim hardware flag LEDs mirror the same colours: yellow = caution ahead, blue = faster car behind, red = session halted/critical error, green = clear after caution, white = final lap (varies by game); flashing used for pit limiter, safety car, system alerts. — [Fanatec FlagLEDs](https://www.fanatec.com/explorer/products/steering-wheel/understanding-fanatec-steering-wheel-flagleds)

- Reading those: deutan green/red delta is still distinguishable (9.4) mainly because of the lightness difference (green L76 vs red L67); making the two more different in lightness (e.g. green L80 #2BE179 and red L68 #FF544C: deutan 10.9) and adding a sign glyph or +/- prefix gives redundancy without layout changes. A blue (#4390FF) vs orange (#FF8B27) delta pair is robust (deutan 35.7) but breaks the green=faster/red=slower convention that sim drivers and F1 broadcast graphics follow; offer it as a theme variant ("CVD-safe"), not the default. Note the F1 timing convention also uses purple for overall-best sector and green for personal-best; the project already uses purple for shift (a different meaning).
- PURPLE (shift) and BLUE (RPM ramp) nearly collapse under protanopia (1.2); the dashboard relies on position/shape (shift-light state) to disambiguate, which is fine, but a lighter/pinker purple or a distinct flash pattern adds redundancy.
- Shift-light order: retrieved sources did not confirm green->red->blue/purple as a convention; I could not cite a source, so treat the current design (blue ramp with purple "shift now") as a project choice, not an established standard.
- The recurring look across the search results is "dark neutral + one saturated accent per meaning + big numerals", which is what Grafite already does; no concrete Pinterest/Dribbble/Behance hex values could be harvested, so third-party palette inspiration should not be cited as evidence.

## 5. How to structure tokens in a single Python theme file (primitive -> semantic alias; swappable themes)

### Takeaway
Use a three-layer structure that mirrors W3C DTCG and the Radix/Carbon/Primer practice: primitives (named ramps, hex), semantic aliases (BACKGROUND, TILE, FASTER...), and per-theme maps that rebind only the aliases; the stdlib can emit both the ARGB strings the layout code needs and a DTCG JSON export for documentation or tooling.

### Cited Findings
- W3C Design Tokens Format Module 2025.10 (latest published version; the page, dated 8 Sept 2026, is a Community Group draft and says it should not be treated as a W3C standard): JSON; a token is an object with `$value`; groups nest; `$type` optional but must resolve (from token, group, or referenced token); `$description` optional; aliases use `{group.token}`; colour tokens use `$type: color` with an object (`colorSpace` e.g. srgb, `components`, optional `hex`, `alpha`); `$root` and `$extends` supported. — [DTCG Format Module](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/drafts/format/)
- Radix recommends a mutable alias (e.g. AppBg) mapped differently per colour mode — [Radix: Understanding the scale](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/understanding-the-scale)
- Carbon ships the same semantic token names across four themes (White, Gray 10, Gray 90, Gray 100), changing only values — [Carbon colour tokens](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/color/tokens/)
- Material 3 derives every theme and contrast level from tonal palettes (same hue/chroma, varying tone) — [Material Color Utilities](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/material-foundation/material-color-utilities)
- Leonardo's approach (target contrast per swatch) is the model for a "contrast-first" generator — [adobe/leonardo](https://github.com/adobe/leonardo)

### Inferences
Three distinct evolution paths for the project (all keep layout untouched, all output `#FFRRGGBB` strings, all stdlib):

- Path A, "Radix-roles, keep the look" (lowest risk). Keep current hexes as primitives but organise them as 12-step ramps per family (neutral "grafite", green, red, amber, blue, ...) with Radix step roles (1 app bg ... 12 text) and make theme.py's existing names (BACKGROUND, PANEL, TILE, TILE_RAISED, SEPARATOR, TRACK, TEXT*) aliases of ramp steps, e.g. BACKGROUND=n1, PANEL=n2, TILE=n3, TILE_RAISED=n4, TRACK=n5, SEPARATOR=n6, TEXT_TERTIARY=n9, TEXT_SECONDARY=n11, TEXT=n12. Optionally seed the neutral ramp from Radix Slate dark hexes (MIT) and the accents' step 9/11 from Radix. Changes: tiny nudges (raise TEXT_TERTIARY to ~Lc 30), formulas keep CSS names where unverified. Benefit: names and roles become a recognised system; cost: accents remain unequal in lightness.

- Path B, "Generated OKLCH ladder" (most systematic). Add a tiny `oklch_to_hex(L, C, h)` (stdlib, with chroma clipping into sRGB) and declare the palette as data: neutral hue 255 / C 0.010 with L steps 14.5, 17.5, 20.5, 24, 27, 29; text L 80/70/56 (+ gear L97); accents on a shared lightness band (GREEN L80, AMBER L82, CYAN L80, ORANGE L75, PURPLE L70, PINK L70, RED L68, BLUE L66, gamut-limited) producing the computed hexes in section 2 (e.g. GREEN #2BE179, RED #FF544C, BLUE #4390FF, ORANGE #FF8B27). Verify each pair with a built-in WCAG/APCA check in a unit test (fail the build if TEXT on TILE < Lc 60 or any accent on TILE < 4.5:1). Pedals keep their "loudest" status by lifting only THROTTLE/BRAKE chroma/L (current L87.9/64.5). Benefit: palette regenerates from 10 numbers, equal-weight accents; cost: look shifts slightly (accents a touch brighter/cooler).

- Path C, "Themeable & CVD-safe" (most flexible). Two layers: `PRIMITIVES` (ramps) and `THEMES = {"graphite": {...}, "oled": {...}, "night": {...}, "cvd": {...}}` where each theme maps the same semantic names to primitives; `theme.py` exports the active theme's names so layout modules are unchanged (select via environment variable or a single constant). Candidate variants (computed): OLED: BG #000000, PANEL #040406, TILE #0C0D10, RAISED #16181B, SEP #242729 (true-black floor; text unchanged); Night/low-glare: gear L ~92, accents reduced chroma; CVD: FASTER/SLOWER = blue #4390FF / orange #FF8B27 (deutan separation 35.7 vs 10.9 for green/red), keep sign glyphs; high-contrast: TEXT toward L90, TERTIARY lifted to Lc 45+. An export function can dump DTCG JSON (`$type: color`, `$value` with colorSpace srgb and hex) for documentation or Figma Tokens use.

Common to all paths: keep alpha prefix `FF` in the semantic layer only (primitives as 6-digit hex), add one function that converts 6-digit to ARGB so layout never sees literals; add a contrast self-check test; keep a distinct hex for formula colours and fall back to CSS names until tested in SimHub.

### Gaps
- DTCG spec is a draft; the colour module (colour-space details) was not fully read (page truncated at 100,000 characters) and tools' support for the 2025.10 colour object varies; no 2026 tool-support survey was done.
- No SimHub-specific guidance on how many theme variants are practical or whether SimHub supports runtime theme switching in Dash Studio (theme here would be a build-time choice in the Python generator).
- Colorbox, Atmos and Huetone export formats not verified.
