# Usability and glanceability of driver-dashboard components (SimHub SF23 DDU, 1280x517, Oct 2026)

Method note: research was done with WebSearch/WebFetch only. The search tool returns summarised snippets; many primary pages returned 403 or were image/binary PDFs (NHTSA 811781, iRacing SF23 manual, ISO 15008 full text). Reddit, Pinterest and Dribbble pages could NOT be retrieved (see Gaps in the relevant sections). Claims below are limited to what a source actually said; calculations are mine and are labelled as Inferences.

## 1. Automotive HMI glance research: NHTSA 2 s / 12 s, ISO 15008, ISO 2575 and minimum character height for 1280x517 at ~70 cm

### Takeaway
NHTSA's visual-manual criteria (single glance about 2 s, 12 s total eyes-off-road per task) were built for infotainment tasks, but they give the right design target for a DDU: every always-visible number must be readable in a single sub-second glance. Legibility standards point to roughly 20-22 arcminutes of visual angle for characters (16-18 acceptable with strong contrast), which at 70 cm is about 4.1-4.5 mm numeral height; ISO 15008 contrast minimums are 3:1 (day/twilight), 5:1 (night), so the #6B717A unit colour is the weakest link in the current palette.

### Cited Findings
- NHTSA's 2013 voluntary guidelines recommend limiting the time a driver must take eyes off the road to perform any task to two seconds at a time and 12 seconds total — [Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/2013/04/25/nhtsa-voluntary-guidelines-distracted-driving/); also summarised by [Canadian Underwriter](https://www.canadianunderwriter.ca/insurance/u-s-government-publishes-guidelines-to-curb-distracted-driving).
- The numbers come from radio-tuning test-track/simulator studies; unrounded data pointed to about 1.3 s per glance and 12.1 s total, rounded to 2.0 and 12.0 s to match occlusion testing (NHTSA report 811781, as relayed by a search summary; the PDF itself returned 403 and could not be read directly) — [NHTSA 811781](https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/811781.pdf).
- The Phase 2 draft extended the same criteria to portable/aftermarket devices ("single average glances away from the forward roadway are 2 seconds or less ... sum ... 12 seconds or less") — [SWOV summary](https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/visual-manual-nhtsa-driver-distraction-guidelines-portable-and-aftermarket-devices).
- Wording differs between sources on the single-glance boundary ("2 s or less" in the Phase 2 draft vs. "not any single glance 2 s or longer within a 6 s window" in a National Academies description of the 2016 version); the second source's page text could not be retrieved, so this is relayed via a search summary only. Verify against the NHTSA primary text before quoting exactly.
- ISO 15008:2017 specifies minimum image quality and legibility for dynamic visual information shown to a passenger-car driver while moving (character legibility, colour recognition); it excludes HUDs, rear-view camera, nav maps and quasi-static items like units or On/Off indicators — [iTeh ISO 15008:2017 catalogue](https://iteh.es/catalog/standards/iso/d07adfe7-f551-4557-bb09-7236d8491cf6/iso-15008-2017).
- ISO 15008:2017 table of contents (preview PDF I could read) confirms dedicated clauses for 4.3.2 minimum contrast ratio, 4.4 colour combinations, 4.5 character dimensions (height, width/height proportion, stroke-width/height weight, spacing, case), 4.8.1 image instability, 4.8.2 image flashing, and 4.9 redundant information displays; it defines "flash" as an intended periodic luminance variation, normally from OFF to a given value, typically used for attracting attention — [ISO 15008:2017 preview PDF](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/62784/ac1daa21f49545c69dc9a5b33259a059/ISO-15008-2017.pdf). The numeric values inside those clauses were not in the preview.
- ISO 15008:2017 minimum symbol-to-background contrast by lighting condition: 5:1 night, 3:1 twilight, 3:1 day, 2:1 direct sunlight (relayed by search summary of the standard; contrast measured per SAE J1757/1). The third edition changed character height and proportion vs the 2009 edition — [iTeh ISO 15008 (standards.iteh.ai)](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/d07adfe7-f551-4557-bb09-7236d8491cf6/iso-15008-2017).
- ISO 9241-303 (ergonomics of displays) quotes "20 to 22 arcminutes of visual angle" for character height; German guidance converts this to viewing distance (mm)/172 = height for 20' and /155 for 22' (e.g. about 2.9-3.2 mm at 500 mm) — [KomNet NRW](https://www.komnet.nrw.de/_sitetools/dialog/13840).
- A Taylor & Francis ergonomics text: character height subtending 20-22 arcmin assures legibility in most cases; 16-18 arcmin may be acceptable depending on factors incl. contrast — [Taylor & Francis chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/e/9781315098128/chapters/10.1201/b22361-52).
- Rules of thumb quoted in the same literature: Bond rule character height = 0.007 x viewing distance (quick-answer displays); FAA-attributed 1/200 of viewing distance (about 17 arcmin) — [Taylor & Francis chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/e/9781315098128/chapters/10.1201/b22361-52) (relayed via search summary). Attribution of "20 arcmin" to the FAA in a classroom paper could not be confirmed.
- SAE AIR1093B (stabilised 2020) is the aviation standard for numeral/letter/symbol dimensions on instrument displays, i.e. the aviation analogue; its values were not retrievable — [SAE AIR1093B](https://saemobilus.sae.org/standards/air1093b-numeral-letter-symbol-dimensions-aircraft-instrument-displays).
- Aviation alert hierarchy (14 CFR 25.1322): red = warning (immediate awareness and response), amber/yellow = caution (immediate awareness, later response), advisory = any colour except red/green; use of red/amber for non-alerting functions must be limited so it does not erode alerting — [Cornell LII 14 CFR 25.1322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/25.1322) (via search summary).
- I found no retrievable text of ISO 2575 (road-vehicle telltale symbols and colours) in this session.

### Inferences
- Angular size arithmetic (mine): 1 arcmin at 700 mm = 0.2036 mm. Heights at 700 mm: 16' = 3.26 mm, 20' = 4.07 mm, 22' = 4.48 mm, 24' (about Bond rule) = 4.9 mm; 1/200 rule = 3.5 mm. At 600 mm: 20' = 3.49 mm; at 900 mm: 20' = 5.24 mm. The 172/155 divisor rule gives 4.07/4.52 mm at 700 mm, consistent.
- Pixel conversion depends on the physical panel width W: pitch = W/1280. For W = 250 / 300 / 350 mm (pitch 0.195 / 0.234 / 0.273 mm): 20' numeral height is about 21 / 17.4 / 14.9 px, 22' is about 23 / 19 / 16.4 px. Ask the user for panel size; if unknown, a safe design rule is: primary numerics (gear, delta, speed) at least 22' (about 19-23 px numeral height, i.e. roughly 28-33 px font size if numeral height is about 0.7 em — assumption, must be measured for Arame Mono), secondary numerics at least 20', labels not below about 16-17' (about 13-16 px height, roughly 18-22 px font) — consistent with "16-18 acceptable at strong contrast".
- Because the screen is wide and short and driver gaze is on track, the practical translation of the 2 s rule: any item needed during braking/cornering should be decodable in one fixation (about 0.3-0.5 s); everything needing more is "straight / pit-only" information.
- Contrast check (my calculation, WCAG formula) on near-black #0A0B0D: white 19.7:1, values #B0B6BD 9.6:1, labels #9AA0A8 7.5:1, units #6B717A 4.0:1 (3.7:1 on #14161A tile fill). So all pass the ISO 15008 3:1 day threshold, but units (and any tile with a lighter fill) fall under the 5:1 night threshold; a dark cockpit room is the typical sim-racing night case. Raising units to about #7F858F or higher (about 5.5:1) fixes it with no layout change.
- ISO 15008 excludes quasi-static items such as units, which is a justification for keeping units dimmer than values, but only down to a point; the 3:1 floor remains good practice.

### Gaps
- Numeric character-height requirement of ISO 15008 clause 4.5.2 and the flashing parameters of 4.8.2: standard text paywalled/preview only. The 20-22' / 16-18' figures come from ISO 9241-303 and an ergonomics textbook, not ISO 15008 itself.
- ISO 2575 telltale colour/symbol conventions: no source retrieved (conventional knowledge, red = danger, amber = caution, green = system active, blue = high beam/information, is NOT cited here).
- NHTSA 811781 and the 2013 Federal Register text could not be fetched directly (403); exact current wording of the glance criteria unverified.
- Physical size of the user's screen is unknown, so the pixel sizes above are a table, not a single answer.

## 2. How real race-car displays prioritise information (F1, SF23, Formula E, AIM/MoTeC/Cosworth/Bosch)

### Takeaway
Real race displays keep a minimal "race page" (gear, speed/RPM via shift lights, delta/lap info), push everything else to pages the driver rarely uses, and use the shift-light bar plus discrete coloured events and dismissible text messages as the alert layer. The user's DDU already mirrors this; what is under-used in the sources is an explicit alarm hierarchy (few real standards are public).

### Cited Findings
- F1/F1 Academy wheel: LED shift lights on the front of the wheel signal upshift at a set RPM; the screen has "20 to 30 pages" but drivers use only two — a main page showing the current gear and a brake-bias page (mainly practice); a Page button cycles pages and holding it resets to page 1 — [F1 Academy: how does the wheel work](https://www.f1academy.com/Latest/2GdyJJMuJ80fxE3ybi46n2/explained-how-does-an-f1-academy-steering-wheel-work).
- Same source: eight front buttons, each colour-coded (green comms, white back/neutral, grey page, yellow mark, red pit limiter, blue rain light, orange start, purple reverse) — colour is used to identify function on the wheel — [F1 Academy](https://www.f1academy.com/Latest/2GdyJJMuJ80fxE3ybi46n2/explained-how-does-an-f1-academy-steering-wheel-work).
- Shift lights double as alerts: pit-limiter engagement flashes the lights, and side lights can repeat flags; the team can push critical messages to the screen — summary of [F1 Academy](https://www.f1academy.com/Latest/2GdyJJMuJ80fxE3ybi46n2/explained-how-does-an-f1-academy-steering-wheel-work), [IONOS x Haas piece](https://drivedigitalsuccess.ionos.com/5-formula-one-steering-wheel-gary-gannon-haas) and [Ferrari SF90 explainer](https://www.ferrari.com/en-BG/magazine/articles/ferrari-sf90-formula-one-steering-wheel) as relayed by search summary (the Ferrari page returned empty on fetch).
- Super Formula SF23 (iRacing manual): the wheel has an integrated digital display with four display pages; the page-by-page contents could not be read because the manual PDF is an image — [iRacing SF23 manual](https://s100.iracing.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Super-Formula-SF23-Manual-V2.pdf).
- Formula E: wheel screen is "the information centre on the wheel" showing tyre pressures, brake temperature, speed, power output, battery status, range and energy consumption; no telemetry to the garage so the driver relies on it — [FIA Formula E: Anatomy of a wheel](https://www.fiaformulae.com/en/news/9924). Attack Mode is armed by a wheel button before the activation zone (25 kW per Audi FE05 explainer; 35 kW per the FIA and Maserati MSG pieces, so figures conflict by era) — [Audi e-tron FE05 explainer](https://www.volkswagengroup.it/en/q-life/inside-the-audi-e-tron-fe05-the-secrets-of-the-formula-e-race-car), [Maserati MSG Racing](https://maseratimsgracing.com/news/racing-academy-get-to-grips-with-the-formula-e-steering-wheel-rokit-venturi-racing). The wheel has acknowledge/reset buttons for warnings and alarms (per search summary).
- MoTeC practice (HPA webinar): on a typical track page "the driver is really going to be ignoring almost everything except for this shift light module"; warning thresholds should be context-dependent (e.g. oil pressure limit as a function of RPM, 40-50 psi at 5000+ RPM vs about 10 psi at idle) to avoid false alarms — [High Performance Academy: Driver warnings](https://www.hpacademy.com/previous-webinars/221-save-your-engine-with-driver-warnings/).
- MoTeC C127 example layout: warnings appear centre-top; low fuel changes the tach bezel colour (orange), lights an icon, and posts a top message (layered redundancy) — [T1 Race Development GT1R C127 layout](https://help.t1racedevelopment.com/support/solutions/articles/4000136020-gt1r-motec-c127-dash-replacement-with-display-creator-layout). The same vendor argues a text message naming the fault beats a generic check-engine lamp.
- AIM dash alarms are configurable by trigger, LED blink rate and colour, dismissal condition, and optional on-screen message — [AIM GS-Dash listing](https://compare.parts/product/22186748651-aim-gs-dash-display/) (via search summary).
- Aviation analogue: three alert levels (warning/caution/advisory) with red/amber mapping — [14 CFR 25.1322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/25.1322).

### Inferences
- Pattern across sources: (1) one dominant "race page"; (2) alerts layered on 2-3 channels (colour change + icon/text + LED/flash); (3) alarms with dismissal conditions so they do not stay forever; (4) thresholds context-dependent. The user's DDU has the layers (15 top-bar warnings, state glows) but should define an explicit 3-level hierarchy (see Q7) so that, e.g., blue flag, limiter, low fuel never fight for the same colour.
- Everything the driver needs during a lap on the wheel pages used in F1 is gear plus a few numbers; the user's right column (4 modes) and left tiles correspond to "pit/straight-only" information and can be made lower-contrast and smaller-weight when the car is cornering/braking (e.g. dim the tile values to 70% brightness at throttle < 100% / brake > 0) without moving them. This is my recommendation, not a sourced practice.
- Public SF23-specific information is thin; the OTS (push-to-pass) colour logic already in the user's gear disc (green active, red cooldown) matches the "arm/active/cooldown" state language of Formula E Attack Mode (arm-before-zone, active, spent), which supports it as a coherent three-state pattern.

### Gaps
- No public documentation found for McLaren/Mercedes wheel page structures, Cosworth or Bosch DDU alarm hierarchies, or SF23 page contents (manual is an image; Ferrari page and f1technical.net returned 403/empty).
- No formal tiered alarm-hierarchy standard for motorsport was found; only vendor examples.
- "What is shown during a lap vs. on straights" is not documented in any retrievable source; the inference above is mine.

## 3. Typography for glanceable numerics (tabular figures, monospace vs proportional, DIN/Eurostile tradition, B612, Atkinson, Inter, Rajdhani, Titillium; is Arame Mono a good choice)

### Takeaway
Fixed-width (tabular) digits are the correct choice for live-changing numbers, so Arame Mono's monospaced digits are structurally right; its weakness is that it is a stencil/sci-fi display face, not a legibility-engineered one. B612 Mono and Atkinson-style disambiguation are the evidence-backed alternatives; the pragmatic path is to keep Arame Mono for large/short strings and test a legibility-first mono (B612 Mono, JetBrains Mono, Inter with tnum) for dense secondary values.

### Cited Findings
- Tabular (monospaced) figures give every digit the same width so dynamically changing numbers do not shift neighbours; proportional figures suit running text; lining figures of uniform height keep columns steadier than oldstyle — [Maersk design system: monospaced numbers](https://designsystem.maersk.com/content/numbers/monospaced-numbers/), [MediaAtelier: When numbers jitter in tables](https://www.mediaatelier.com/en-CH/Posts/Tabular-Figures), [VideoLAN issue 351](https://code.videolan.org/videolan/vlc-ios/-/issues/351).
- Arame Mono is a monospaced member of the Arame family by Dimitre Lima; described as a stencil/typewriter/display sans, reportedly updated for the Iron Man HUD in The Avengers and used in the Halo 4 interface; design 2004, released 2005, upgraded Nov 2011; Fontspring lists 4 styles (Hitype), MyFonts says 8 (conflict) — [Fontspring Arame Mono](https://fontspring.com/fonts/hitype/0arame-mono), [MyFonts Arame](https://myfonts.com/fonts/dimitrelima/0arame).
- B612 was designed for Airbus cockpit displays (ENAC / University of Toulouse III / Airbus, 2010 onward; type design by intactile DESIGN: Nicolas Chauveau, Thomas Paillot, with Jean-Luc Vinot). Design principles: identification (retain natural stroke contrast, accentuated ascenders/descenders, non-rectangular bowls to keep counterforms) and discrimination (maximise distances between character forms). Experiments on successive versions produced "confusion matrices", but the project page reports no quantified results — [ENAC LII project page](https://lii.enac.fr/projects/definition-and-validation-of-an-aeronautical-font/), [Fedora wiki: Polarsys B612](https://fedoraproject.org/wiki/Polarsys_B612_fonts).
- B612 comes as B612 and B612 Mono (Regular, Italic, Bold, Bold Italic), open source under EPL 1.0/EDL 1.0 — [Fedora wiki](https://fedoraproject.org/wiki/Polarsys_B612_fonts), [Google Fonts B612 Mono](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/B612%2BMono/about). Claims about reduced fatigue/cognitive load are from the font makers; no independent test found.
- Atkinson Hyperlegible (Braille Institute) emphasises letterform distinction over uniformity: disambiguates 0/O, B/8, 1/I/l with exaggerated forms, opened counters, angled spurs, differentiated tails; tested with people with varying visual challenges; free under SIL OFL; "Next" version (2025) has seven weights — [Adobe Express blog](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/atkinson-hyperlegible-font), [Google Fonts GitHub](https://github.com/googlefonts/atkinson-hyperlegible), [Adobe Fonts: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next](https://fonts.adobe.com/fonts/atkinson-hyperlegible-next).
- The SF23 iRacing manual itself is set with an Eurostile Extended Black font (embedded font name "EurostileExt-Bla" in the PDF metadata) — evidence the "Eurostile" motorsport aesthetic is current in the SF23 brand, not evidence about the in-car display font — [iRacing SF23 manual](https://s100.iracing.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Super-Formula-SF23-Manual-V2.pdf).
- SimHub community feedback on graphics-vs-use: "tiny graphics look good in screenshots but are not very useful mid-race if you have to strain your eyes"; recommends big gear and revs — [SimXPro guide](https://simxpro.com/blogs/guides/simhub-for-sim-racing-dashboards-overlays-and-the-why-behind-it).

### Inferences
- Arame Mono verdict: (a) positive: fixed-width digits stop jitter; wide advance (0.6 em) gives air between digits; distinctive HUD character. (b) risks: stencil/open-contour display design is meant for large display sizes and has no published legibility testing or confusion-matrix evidence; small sizes (labels, units) lose letter identification; the 0.6 em advance wastes horizontal room in the narrow tiles, forcing smaller point sizes (bad for 20' targets). Recommendation: keep Arame Mono at large sizes (gear, delta, speed, lap times) and consider a legibility-engineered fallback for 16-22 px text.
- Candidate comparison (my judgement; only B612 and Atkinson have sourced rationale here): B612 Mono is purpose-designed for cockpit screens (sourced); JetBrains Mono, Inter (tnum), DIN-like (Rajdhani, Titillium) were NOT supported by retrievable evidence in this session, so they should be tested empirically (mock-up at actual px size, view from 70 cm) rather than asserted as better.
- "Eurostile/DIN tradition" claim (squarish, technical forms) could not be sourced; treat as stylistic, not evidence of legibility.
- Letter-spacing: since Arame Mono is already monospaced, extra tracking is likely harmful for values (digits already spaced 0.6 em); tracking should be used only on all-caps labels (a common practice; not sourced here).
- Weight: since #B0B6BD on near-black has 9.6:1 contrast, light text on dark "blooms" (halation), so slightly heavier weight is not needed; instead raise size before weight (my inference).
- Real-time numbers: confirm the delta, speed and lap-time fields use tabular digits and a fixed field width with a reserved sign slot, to avoid horizontal jitter.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed comparison of DIN/Eurostile/Rajdhani/Titillium/JetBrains Mono/Inter for glance reading found.
- No quantified B612 legibility results (confusion matrices not published on the page I could read).
- No testing evidence on Arame Mono legibility at small sizes.
- Inter Display and Funnel Sans: no evidence retrieved.

## 4. Visual hierarchy, pre-attentive attributes, blinking, colour-blind redundancy, delta-bar conventions, gauge vs number vs bar

### Takeaway
Use size and luminance for the first read, colour only as a second channel, and flash sparingly with a clear on/off contrast; every colour state should have a redundant cue (shape, text, position) for colour-vision deficiencies. Delta bars are conventionally green/red (faster/slower), which is exactly the pair colour-blind users confuse, so shape/position redundancy is essential.

### Cited Findings
- ISO 15008:2017 explicitly treats "image flashing" (4.8.2), "image instability" (4.8.1), "colour combinations" (4.4) and "redundant information displays" (4.9) as requirement areas; flash is defined as intended periodic luminance variation normally from OFF to a value, used for attracting attention — [ISO 15008:2017 preview](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/62784/ac1daa21f49545c69dc9a5b33259a059/ISO-15008-2017.pdf). The permitted flash frequencies were not visible.
- No human-factors standard value for blink rate/duty cycle could be retrieved; patents describe about 2 Hz as the upper end of a normal vehicle hazard/signal flash and say attention capture depends on clear light-dark contrast, not rate alone — these are patent statements, not validated guidance — [USPTO patent 11135968](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11135968).
- Aviation alerting separates levels by colour and urgency (red warning, amber caution, advisory not red/green) and limits red/amber use for non-alerts so the alert colours keep their meaning — [14 CFR 25.1322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/25.1322).
- Okabe-Ito guidance: use a colour-vision-deficiency-safe palette and redundant visual coding (e.g. solid vs dashed as well as colour); never rely on red-green alone; stricter checks for "hallway glance"/control-room viewing — [factoextra palette docs (Okabe-Ito)](https://rdrr.io/cran/factoextra/man/factoextra_palette.html), [Reed College / ProfHacker colour-blind figures](https://www.reed.edu/economics/parker/311/Creating-Color-Blind-Accessible-Figures-ProfHacker---Blogs---The-Chronicle-of-Higher-Education.pdf), [Clickport chart colours](https://clickport.io/docs/chart-colors) (via search summary; the "distinct luminance" claim was unverified).
- Delta convention in SimHub community dashes: bar turns red when slower than best and green when faster (Rietveld GT3 dash) — [SimHub Dash: Rietveld dash](https://www.simhubdash.com/community-2/dashboard-templates/iracing-dash-from-rietveld-design/); Tritium dash v2.0.0 darkened the background of the positive (red) delta "to make it easier to read" and enlarged lap times/sectors for readability — [Tritium dash release 2.0.0](https://github.com/Tritium-Sim-Racing/tritium-dash/releases/tag/v2.0.0).
- A sim-dash guide lists delta among the first items to show (after gear, RPM, fuel) and advises simplifying if reading the dash takes more than a split second — [SimRacingCockpit guide](https://simracingcockpit.gg/sim-racing-dashboard/) (via search summary).
- Overlaying left/right radar on the centre module gives a visible alert only when a car is alongside while keeping gauges visible otherwise (Tritium) — [Tritium release 2.0.0](https://github.com/Tritium-Sim-Racing/tritium-dash/releases/tag/v2.0.0).

### Inferences
- Pre-attentive attributes (general knowledge, not sourced here): size, luminance contrast, motion/flash and a single saturated hue pop out; combining >1 hue for a single state weakens pop-out. In the current design, the gear disc already uses 5 glow colours; that works only if each has a distinct secondary cue: e.g. shift = purple + outline thickens, OTS active = green + small "OTS" caption, cooldown = red + radial/segment fill, limiter = cyan + flashing "LIM", so state is decodable without hue.
- Colour-blind redundancy plan fitting the constraints (no repositioning): delta = sign glyph (+/−) and arrow/triangle shape plus colour; brake bias flash = "▲/▼" or "+/−" tick text plus green/red; comb bars = tens marked already (good); purple vs blue on shift LEDs differ in luminance — verify with a CVD simulator (e.g. Vischeck) and ensure the shift LEDs also change count/size, not just hue.
- Blink policy (my recommendation, flagged unsourced for rate): reserve blinking for events requiring action within seconds (limiter, damage, shift-now, blue flag), use 2 Hz-ish with about 50% duty and stop after N seconds or on acknowledgement; use a single static colour change for caution-level states; avoid simultaneous blinks in more than one region; keep blinking confined to the element's own box so peripheral vision picks it up without scanning.
- Number vs gauge vs bar: numbers for precise values read once (lap time, delta number, fuel), bars for rate-of-change/trend (delta progress comb, balance bar, OTS) — consistent with how the user's layout already splits delta number and bar; add a small rate-of-change cue to the number (colour of trend or ▲▼) instead of a new gauge (inference, no direct source).
- Visual hierarchy ladder to apply inside existing boxes: Level 1 (white or brightest, largest): gear, delta number, active alert; Level 2 (#B0B6BD, large): speed, RPM, lap times; Level 3 (#B0B6BD, medium): tile values; Level 4 (#9AA0A8): labels; Level 5 (raise #6B717A to about #7F858F): units. Also vary size ratio by at least about 1.5x between adjacent levels.

### Gaps
- No authoritative blink-rate/duty-cycle guidance retrieved (ISO 15008 4.8.2 text paywalled; AC 25.1322-1 not retrieved).
- No academic study found on delta-bar designs in racing displays.
- Pre-attentive attribute claims are general HCI knowledge, not tied to a retrieved source.

## 5. Information density: what sim racers complain about and praise (Lovely Dashboard, DahlDesign, TWF, RSS)

### Takeaway
Retrievable evidence is thin and secondary: the consistent message is "few items, large, near the sight line", with praise for clarity and complaints about small fonts and clutter. Reddit threads themselves could not be retrieved, so no direct Reddit quotes are available.

### Cited Findings
- Guides advise a small, focused dash; larger displays with more fields need more space and precise positioning to avoid clutter; recognise crucial information with minimal eye movement — [SimXPro guide](https://simxpro.com/blogs/guides/simhub-for-sim-racing-dashboards-overlays-and-the-why-behind-it), [SimRacingCockpit guide](https://simracingcockpit.gg/sim-racing-dashboard/).
- "Readability is key: a good dashboard prioritises big gear, clear revolutions and essential data in a generous size"; tiny graphics are not useful mid-race — [SimXPro guide](https://simxpro.com/blogs/guides/simhub-for-sim-racing-dashboards-overlays-and-the-why-behind-it).
- A SimHub user made a widget large "because otherwise I can't read it on my 27 inch screen"; another said a leaderboard font was too small — [SimHub Dash community pages](https://www.simhubdash.com/participant/simbob/activity/) (via search summary).
- A review of a 10.3" dash: its size keeps delta readable at a glance, but it is large for non-ultrawide setups — [Sim-Lab Porsche 911 GT3 Cup DDU review (SimRacingSetup)](https://simracingsetup.com/review/sim-lab-porsche-911-gt3-cup-ddu/).

## 7. Alert/warning design: blue flag, pit limiter, fuel low, damage, track limits without overload; telltale conventions

### Takeaway
Use a small number of alert levels, assign one colour per level, layer a persistent cue (colour/icon) with a transient one (text/flash), and make alerts expire or be dismissible; reserve flashing for urgent items. Real systems pair the shift-light bar with event flashes (pit limiter) and use text naming the fault.

### Cited Findings
- Pit limiter engagement flashes the F1 shift lights; side LEDs can repeat track flags — [F1 Academy](https://www.f1academy.com/Latest/2GdyJJMuJ80fxE3ybi46n2/explained-how-does-an-f1-academy-steering-wheel-work), [IONOS x Haas piece](https://drivedigitalsuccess.ionos.com/5-formula-one-steering-wheel-gary-gannon-haas).
- Low fuel on a MoTeC layout: tach bezel turns orange, icon lights, and a top-centre message appears (layered cues) — [T1 Race Development](https://help.t1racedevelopment.com/support/solutions/articles/4000136020-gt1r-motec-c127-dash-replacement-with-display-creator-layout).
- AIM alarms define trigger, LED blink rate/colour, dismissal conditions and optional message — [AIM GS-Dash](https://compare.parts/product/22186748651-aim-gs-dash-display/).
- Alarm thresholds should depend on context (RPM) to avoid nuisance alarms; a track page should otherwise be quiet — [HPA webinar](https://www.hpacademy.com/previous-webinars/221-save-your-engine-with-driver-warnings/).
- Named-fault text is more informative than generic lamps — [T1 Race Development](https://help.t1racedevelopment.com/support/solutions/articles/4000136020-gt1r-motec-c127-dash-replacement-with-display-creator-layout).
- Warning/caution/advisory definitions and red/amber restrictions — [14 CFR 25.1322](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/25.1322).
- F1 wheel buttons use consistent function colours: red pit limiter, blue rain light, etc. — [F1 Academy](https://www.f1academy.com/Latest/2GdyJJMuJ80fxE3ybi46n2/explained-how-does-an-f1-academy-steering-wheel-work).
- Formula E wheel has acknowledge and reset buttons for warnings/alarms (search summary of FE wheel explainers) — [Audi FE05 explainer](https://www.volkswagengroup.it/en/q-life/inside-the-audi-e-tron-fe05-the-secrets-of-the-formula-e-race-car).
- Overlaying radar alert on the centre module shows alerts only when relevant — [Tritium 2.0.0](https://github.com/Tritium-Sim-Racing/tritium-dash/releases/tag/v2.0.0).

### Inferences
- Proposed hierarchy for the 15 top-bar warnings (my proposal, built on the sourced aviation/MoTeC patterns): Level A "act now" (red, flashing, plus icon and word): pit limiter missing/engine damage/stall; Level B "act soon" (amber, steady or slow pulse, icon plus word): fuel low, damage, track-limits/penalty; Level C "information" (blue/cyan or neutral, steady, auto-expire in 3-5 s): blue flag, yellow flag, rain. Because blue flag also means "let car pass" it should not use red; add a distinct icon (diagonal split flag), not only colour. Pit limiter is a state, not a fault: use steady cyan/white-on-blue badge with "PIT LIM" text, flashing only when speed exceeds the limit.
- To avoid overload: cap simultaneous top-bar alerts to one plus a counter ("+2"), queue by level, and suppress low levels during Level A. The gear disc glow should not duplicate top-bar alerts: if the gear glow already signals limiter (cyan), omit a second flashing element.
- Colour reuse conflict risk: the design uses red for OTS cooldown, bad delta and brake-bias decrease; aviation rule says red/amber must be limited to alerts to preserve meaning. Consider desaturating OTS cooldown red (or using grey with a progress sweep) so red remains "warning".
- Telltale conventions (ISO 2575) could not be sourced; if used, verify symbol and colour against the standard.
